The one rule that outranks the rest

There are two violets in this repository and they must never meet. The application's identity is #5B47D6 (light) / #7060e0 (dark), defined in app/globals.css. The generated deck's violet is #6D4AF5, defined in lib/branding/brand.ts. They are separate systems with separate grounds, separate radii and separate rules. This document governs the first one only.

The single sanctioned crossing is the slide-thumbnail strip in presentation-preview.tsx, which paints deck ground #F3F2F2 and deck violet #6D4AF5 because it is depicting the deck. Nothing else in the app may reference a deck token.

Design language

A quiet tool that gets loud only where evidence needs pointing at.

uxproof is a working instrument, not a marketing surface. The chrome recedes; the research content is the only thing with weight.

Violet is the only chroma, and it is rationed

Neutrals are violet-tinted rather than grey — surfaces are #f8f7fc, borders are rgba(91,71,214,.10) — so the accent feels native rather than applied. Saturated violet marks exactly three things: the primary action, the active state, and work in progress.

Flat by default, elevated only when detached

Surfaces separate by tint and a hairline, not by shadow. Shadows appear only on things that genuinely float: dropdowns, popovers, toasts, the open history drawer.

Compact density, generous page rhythm

Controls are small (32 px default button, 32 px input) and gaps inside a row are tight (6–12 px), but sections are separated by 24–32 px and page gutters are 24 px. Dense where you work, open where you read.

Text carries the hierarchy; boxes rarely do

One display headline per view at text-3xl with -0.04em tracking, a muted one-line subtitle, then content. Card titles are font-medium, not bold. Nothing shouts twice.

Motion narrates, it never decorates

Every animation reports state: the step ladder advancing, a toast counting down its own lifetime, a view fading in from a 4 px blur. All of it is suppressed under prefers-reduced-motion.

The interface is honest about what it knows

Every number shown came from an uploaded file. Empty states say upload something rather than showing a plausible demo; deletions name exactly what leaves. The visual language serves that: no filler cards, no padded grids, no invented data.

Rules that follow from the measurements

  • 1 · Every text pair clears AA in both themes, and it is meant to stay that way. Two values moved in v1.1: --muted-foreground darkened from #717182 (4.49:1 on card), and the dark --destructive pair — 2.66:1, which made form errors almost invisible — was inverted.
  • 2 · Emerald and amber are icon-only in light mode. At 3.77:1 and 3.19:1 they clear the 3:1 bar for graphics but not the 4.5:1 bar for text. Every use is a dot, a check glyph or a 14–16px icon — if a label must read as successful, use foreground and let the icon carry the hue.
  • 3 · Muted text on a card is the floor (5.06:1), and it is where most muted text lands. Never introduce a lighter muted, and never put muted text on muted (4.62:1 is already the tightest pairing the system allows).

The signature easing is cubic-bezier(0.22, 1, 0.36, 1) — a fast, decelerating settle. Use it for anything that enters. Exits reuse the entrance in reverse and stay under 200ms.

Reduced motion is a hard requirement

@media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce) in globals.css collapses every animation and transition to 0.01ms and disables smooth scrolling; <MotionConfig reducedMotion="user"> in app-shell.tsx covers framer-motion. Any new animation must be reachable by one of those two — never animate through an inline style or a JS timer that neither can suppress. Corollary: no animation may be the sole carrier of information. The step ladder also changes text colour and swaps its dot for a check; the toast also prints the seconds remaining as digits.

Button sizes

xs · 24 sm · 28 default · 32 lg · 36 icon · 32²

default (32px) is the standard. sm is for dense rows — file-row actions, top-bar nav, follow-up chips. xs exists but is unused in the product. Icon-only buttons are perfect squares at the same heights and must carry an aria-label.

Applied action rules

Navigation is a button whose fill means “you are here”

The top-bar items switch ghost → default on the active route rather than using an underline or a rail. It is the same component doing double duty as state.

Destructive actions are ghost until confirmed

A delete affordance is a ghost icon button that turns text-red-500 with a bg-red-500/10 wash on hover. The only solid red button in the product is the confirm inside ConfirmDialog — red fill is earned by the confirmation step, not offered before it.

Row actions hide until the row is engaged

Drawer rename and delete sit at opacity-0, revealed by group-hover and focus-within — a hover-only affordance is unreachable by keyboard.

Icons are 16px, 14px in dense rows, and always paired with a label

Lucide, stroke-width 2, inheriting currentColor. The exception is the accent-tinted leading icon on outline buttons (violet Sparkles, Plus) marking an AI action. Icon-only is allowed in chrome only, with a tooltip and an aria-label.

Composite action specimens

File-row pair — outline (secondary) then filled (primary)

Summarize Generate presentation

Reading order left to right: alternative, primary, then the quiet destructive and disclosure controls pushed to the far right. Never place a delete next to a primary.

State semantics

focus-visible — a 3px ring/50 halo plus a violet border, identical on every variant and never removed. hover — every variant responds, the filled one at bg-primary/80; until v1.1 that rule was scoped to [a], so a real <button> never changed. active — down 1px, except on menu triggers. loading — spinner in place of the leading icon, a present-participle label (“Deleting…”), disabled. Fix the recipe, never the call site: the two per-call-site hover overrides that existed to paper over the gap were deleted with it.

Form behaviour, product-wide

  • · Validate on submit, not on keystroke. Errors clear the moment the user edits the field again.
  • · One error line per form, role="alert", text-sm text-destructive, placed directly above the submit button. Fields are not individually annotated.
  • · Client checks are a courtesy; the route is the authority. The login screen says so in a comment and the server repeats every check.
  • · Submit stays disabled until the form can succeed — and, where the form edits existing values (Profile), until something has actually changed (profileDirty).
  • · Every field has a real <Label htmlFor> and a correct autoComplete. Placeholders are examples, never labels.

Removed, and why it stays removed

tabs.tsx, accordion.tsx, sheet.tsx and chart.tsx were styled, exported and imported nowhere; v1.1 deleted all four, and recharts went with the chart primitive. The product's only disclosure is the hand-rolled chevron on a file row and its only tab-like control is the sign-in switch — if either pattern recurs, reinstate one primitive and retire the hand-rolled version rather than adding a third. Chart in particular should not come back: the charts this product makes are drawn into the deck, not the page. progress.tsx and separator.tsx survive unused — both are dependency-free, and they are the right thing to reach for instead of hand-rolling a bar or a divider.

tabs.tsx, accordion.tsx, sheet.tsx and chart.tsx are styled and exported but imported nowhere. They are not part of the current design language and should not be treated as precedent. Two consequences: the product's only “accordion” is the hand-rolled chevron disclosure on a file row, and its only tab-like control is the hand-rolled sign-in / create-account switch — if a real need for either arises, adopt the primitive and delete the hand-rolled one rather than adding a third pattern. chart.tsx also drags in recharts: there is no chart anywhere in the app, because the charts this product makes are drawn in the deck.

Interaction & UX rules14

Actions

Emphasis is a budget, not a style choice

Exactly one filled button per view or dialog. Alternatives are outline; chrome is ghost. If a screen seems to need two primaries, it needs two screens — or one of them is not primary.

Destructive actions are confirmed, always, and only once

Every deletion routes through ConfirmDialog. There is no undo and no “are you sure?” snackbar — the confirmation happens before, not after. Non-destructive actions are never confirmed; the app does not ask permission to do what was just asked of it.

A disabled control owes an explanation

Every disabled in the codebase pairs with a title saying what is missing. Disabling silently is the one interaction the product treats as a defect.

Expensive work waits for a click

Deck generation auto-starts only for a genuinely fresh reply (under 60s old, not restored from history, not an upload follow-up). Restored conversations never fire background work. An upload offers the deck; it does not start building one.

Feedback

All confirmation is a toast, in one column

Under the avatar, top-16 right-4, max 10s, pausable, dismissable. No per-view banners, no inline success text, no alert dialogs for success.

Say what happened, not that something happened

“Deleted q3-usability.pdf and its Q3 2025 data”, not “Deleted successfully”. Where an action has consequences the user did not name, the confirmation names them.

Long work is narrated by stage

Multi-second operations show the named pipeline steps with per-step timings, not a progress bar and not a spinner. Sub-second operations show nothing.

Errors are quiet, specific and local

A failure renders where it happened — an error bubble in the thread, a destructive card state, one role="alert" line above a form's submit. Never a modal. Retry is offered wherever retrying could work.

Sign-in failures say one thing

“No such account” and “wrong password” return the same message, by design — the form must not become an account-enumeration oracle. This is a product rule that constrains the UI copy, and it outranks helpfulness.

Navigation & surfaces

The URL is the source of truth

There is no currentView flag. The top bar reads usePathname() and pushes routes; the active nav fill is derived from the path. Selecting a session from a non-chat route navigates for real, because the shallow pushState the store uses only works while a chat surface is mounted.

The wordmark means “start over”

Clicking it opens a fresh session at /. It is not a home link to a marketing page — there isn't one.

Two kinds of modal, chosen by consequence

Anchored under the avatar for settings and profile — reversible, ambient, and visibly connected to the account menu. Screen-centred for confirmations — irreversible, and meant to interrupt. Never anchor a decision; never centre a preference.

Chrome persists; views swap

AppShell holds the top bar, drawer, panels and toaster across every route. Pages stay server components rendering a thin *-view.tsx; all state and animation live in components/. Views enter with a 200ms opacity-and-blur fade — the only route transition in the product.

Theme is explicit, light by default

One toggle in the top bar showing the mode you would move to, persisted to localStorage. The OS preference is consulted only on a first visit with nothing stored. Every colour decision must therefore be written as a light dark: pair.

Honesty rules that shape the UI

Product invariants that decide what may be drawn:

  • · Nothing on screen is invented. Every figure comes from a file the signed-in user uploaded. The interface therefore has no placeholder data, no sample dashboard and no demo mode — an account with no uploads sees an empty state asking for a file.
  • · Measurements that were not taken are not shown. The ⚡ 0.0s case is filtered out of the message metadata rather than printed.
  • · A preview is labelled as a preview. The slide strip stays representative until the deck exists.
  • · Deleting data means deleting it. The UI never hides a record it still keeps, and the confirmation names every downstream thing that goes with it.

The product has not been formally evaluated against a WCAG conformance level, and this document makes no such claim. What follows is the set of practices the code already holds to, stated as rules, plus the gaps that a future audit would find first.

Colour and contrast

Measured ratios are on page 04. As of v1.1 every text pair in the palette clears 4.5:1 in both themes — two values moved to get there, and the two that remain below it (emerald and amber) are icon-only by rule. Treat muted-foreground on card (5.06:1) as the floor. Separately: colour is never the only signal. Pipeline steps also change dot shape and text decoration; toasts also carry a distinct icon; the active nav item is a fill change, not a hue change.

Known gaps — fix these before claiming conformance

1 · Touch targets are below the 44×44 guidance

Icon buttons are 24–32px and row actions 24px. Acceptable for a pointer-driven desktop tool, and consistent with the product's density — but it is a real gap, and it is the reason no mobile layout should be “added” without revisiting control sizing.

2 · title is doing explanatory work

Disabled controls explain themselves through the native title attribute, which is inconsistently exposed by assistive technology and unavailable to keyboard users. The explanation should move into visible helper text or an aria-describedby target.

3 · The sign-in switch is a partial tab implementation

It has role="tablist", role="tab" and aria-selected, but no tabpanel, no aria-controls and no arrow-key navigation. Complete it — or drop the tab roles for aria-pressed toggles, which is closer to what it actually is. (The Tabs primitive was deleted as dead code in v1.1.)

4 · Streaming text is not announced

Assistant replies reveal character by character with no live region, so a screen reader gets no notification that an answer has arrived. A aria-live="polite" region announcing the completed message is the minimal fix.

5 · Emerald/amber are one careless label away from failing

Confined to icons, which clear the 3:1 graphics threshold. v1.1 deleted the one place they were paired with text — an unrendered feature list in welcome-empty-state.tsx. Guard this in review; the rule is in CLAUDE.md.

6 · No skip link past the fixed chrome

Keyboard users traverse the top bar on every route. A skip-to-content link is cheap and currently absent.

Checklist for any new component

  • ☐ Reachable and operable by keyboard alone, in a sensible order
  • ☐ Focus is visible using the standard ring — not a custom one, and never removed
  • ☐ It is a real control element, or a Radix primitive; no clickable div
  • ☐ Icon-only? It has an aria-label
  • ☐ Stateful? The state is in the DOM (aria-expanded / -current / -selected / -invalid)
  • ☐ Colour is not the only difference between two states
  • ☐ Text meets the contrast floor on the surface it actually sits on — check against card, not background
  • ☐ Danger uses destructive, never a raw red-* or rose-*
  • ☐ Any animation is suppressed under prefers-reduced-motion
  • ☐ Anything announced asynchronously sits in a live region
  • ☐ Disabled? The reason is visible, not only in a title

v1.0 findings — resolved in the code

v1.0 of this document catalogued eight inconsistencies in the shipped system. Six are now fixed in code rather than described; two remain open and are stated as rules.

✓ Three reds became one token

Every red-* and rose-* utility is gone. Validation, error bubbles, error toasts, delete affordances and the confirm button all read --destructive / --destructive-foreground, with /5–/20 washes. The dark pair was inverted on the way through — it scored 2.66:1 and made form errors almost invisible.

✓ Eleven dead tokens deleted

All eight --sidebar-* tokens plus --input-background and --switch-background, in both themes, along with the --color-sidebar-* mappings in @theme inline. The drawer is not a separately-themed surface, and a second surface palette would fracture the system.

✓ Seven dead files deleted

Three product components — presentation-download-button.tsx (painted in a zinc/emerald palette belonging to neither theme), ai-processing-state.tsx, pipeline-status.tsx — and four unused primitives (tabs, accordion, sheet, chart). recharts went with the chart primitive; removing it also surfaced immer as an undeclared direct dependency of the store, now declared.

✓ The primary button hovers

Its hover rule was scoped to [a], so a real <button> never responded. Fixed in the cva recipe — and the two per-call-site overrides that existed to paper over it were deleted, which is the general rule: fix the recipe, never the call site.

✓ Contrast raised to AA throughout

--muted-foreground darkened from #717182 to #696979, clearing 4.5:1 on background, card and muted alike. The only pairs still under it are emerald and amber, which are icon-only by rule — and the one place they were paired with a text label, an unrendered feature list, is gone.

◆ Dashboard gradients — scoped exception

The eight hashed multi-hue gradients stay: they give each deck a stable, recognisable cover in a grid of otherwise identical cards, and the hue carries no meaning. The exception is now written at the definition in dashboard.tsx and in CLAUDE.md — decorative cover for a generated artefact only, never chrome, status, data or type.

○ Still open — two heading systems

The base layer styles bare h1/h2/h3, but every page headline sets display text-3xl explicitly, so the base rule almost never applies. Treat the base layer as a safety net for markdown-ish content and always set the role explicitly in components.

○ Still open — duplicated password field

The same PasswordInput is defined in login-screen.tsx and profile-dialog.tsx, differing only by a bg-muted/30. One component in components/ui/, with the panel fill as a prop.
